Description

In this core studio-seminar, students are introduced to the fundamentals of experimental interaction design. Atelier 1 builds on the practical and technical skills gained in SCTM-2005 Intro to Comp Sci - Logic & Coding. It investigates the historical, conceptual, and technical methods needed to create engaging interactive experiences for people, places, or situations. Students learn how coding can be combined with a variety of media to question current assumptions of how and why interactive systems work. The course will present tools and methods that allow projects to be tested early and often to understand how this impacts the overall creative process.

Academic Integrity

Academic integrity is a shared responsibility held by all members of the OCAD University community. We uphold the integrity of knowledge-making and creativity: this means obtaining and sharing information responsibly, being respectful and transparent in the way that we use and respond to the work and contributions of others and explicitly acknowledging how such work and contributions inform our own. The University is committed to enforcing the policy and all allegations of academic misconduct will therefore be reported and investigated. The most common type of academic misconduct is plagiarism, that is, deliberately misrepresenting someone else’s words, ideas or original visual concepts as your own in written or visual work. Other examples of academic misconduct include misuse of written or visual sources (“unintentional plagiarism”), cheating during an examination or test, collaborating on assignments without permission, and submitting any work for evaluation that has previously been submitted in another course without prior approval. Read the Academic Misconduct policy to ensure you are well informed.

Academic Integrity and Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI)

The Academic Misconduct policy includes specific guidance (rules/recommendations) around Generative AI (GAI) applications (e.g. Sora, Midjourney, ChatGPT, Replit). GAI can be a powerful tool for research and creative practice, but may not always be relevant or appropriate for use in a course. Use of a GAI application can be a form of academic misconduct. As a student, you have a responsibility to follow the parameters (rules) set by your instructor for the use of GAI in your course. Parameters for acceptable GAI use vary from course to course and even assignment to assignment. Consult your course outline and assignment instructions for guidance on how these applications may be used and how they should be attributed (referenced) when you do use them. When unclear or in doubt, reach out to your course instructor for clarification.

Commitment to Sustainability

OCAD University is committed to developing decolonial approaches to sustainability that foster environmental justice through interdisciplinary solutions. To address the climate crisis, you are encouraged to approach your art, design, creative and critical practices with an understanding of the interconnections among environmental, social, economic and cultural activities. You may also consult the OCAD U Sustainability Policy which builds on the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (UNSDGs) in support of ecologically-informed and holistic thinking that cultivates relationships between people, place, and land. Together we can advance global sustainability goals which include, among others: reduced inequalities; good health and well-being; affordable and clean energy; clean water and sanitation; climate action; and responsible consumption and production.
